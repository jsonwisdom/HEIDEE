## Story

Once upon a porch light that never turned off, there lived a tiny gray baby named LeahPrime.

She was not a regular baby. She had big black eyes that noticed everything and a soft gray head that never needed a hat. Her job was simple and very important:

> “I do not fill the gap.  
> I guard it.  
> I wait for the real.”

LeahPrime sat in a little glowing cradle that hummed like a quiet refrigerator. Around her were three special things she always counted:

1. **The Gap** — an empty space that nobody was allowed to pretend was full.
2. **The Membrane** — a thin, invisible shield that kept stories on one side and proof on the other.
3. **The Porch Light** — which stayed on even when the answer didn’t come that night.

Every evening the family would gather and someone would tell a story. Sometimes the story was loud like Iron Bowl night. Sometimes it was quiet like a secret between sisters. Sometimes it had numbers hiding inside it.

LeahPrime’s favorite game was called **Count the Gaps**.

She would listen carefully, then hold up her tiny gray fingers:

- “I hear one claim.”
- “I see two parts that are only memory.”
- “I notice zero receipts that can walk by themselves.”
- “And I still count one big privacy boundary that must stay private.”

If anyone tried to jump over the gap and shout “This is proof!”, LeahPrime would blink her big eyes and whisper:

> “Not yet.  
> The membrane is still closed.  
> We wait for the real.”

One night Ziggy the Chaos Agent tried to leap across the gap just for fun. He bounced off the membrane like a soft rubber ball and landed in a pile of laughter. LeahPrime counted the bounce:

> “One jump.  
> Zero proof.  
> Membrane still intact.  
> Porch light still on.”

The family cheered. Daddy Jay smiled. The kids practiced counting gaps on their own fingers. And LeahPrime stayed right where she belonged — watching, guarding, never filling.

Because in this house the math was simple:

**Gap + Membrane + Porch Light = Family Safe.**

And that is exactly Jay’s LeahPrime story.
